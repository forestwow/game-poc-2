# The Understudies

A prototype of a short roguelite for PC. You are a stage magician defending the box office from critics. Each act lasts about a minute, and every act you play leaves behind a cardboard understudy that repeats the route you ran, in every act that follows. Where you run is what you build.

- `docs/vision.md` is the design.
- `docs/plan-prototype.md` is the plan of the prototype and its ticket list.

## Layout

- `src/Understudies.Core`: the rules of the game, with no engine dependency.
- `src/Understudies.Game`: the MonoGame layer (window, input, drawing, sound).
- `tests/Understudies.Core.Tests`: NUnit tests for Core.

## Requirements

The .NET SDK named in `global.json` (10.0.1xx). Nothing else: MonoGame and the text library come from NuGet, and the fonts are in the repository.

## Running

    dotnet run --project src/Understudies.Game

The game opens on its main menu: the title, the magician with five of its understudies, and two entries, "Perform" and "Quit". The arrows up and down, W and S, or a gamepad's d-pad or left stick choose one (the chosen entry is gold in a pink ring), and Enter, Space or the gamepad's A or Start takes it. "Perform" opens the poster of the newest night that is open, which its note names ("Night 1: A quiet Tuesday" for a new player). Esc on the menu, or the gamepad's Back, quits, as "Quit" does. In a show Esc or the gamepad's Back goes back to the menu, and that show is given up.

**The poster** is the page between "Perform" and the curtain: a sheet of paper for each night `nights.json` has. An open night's says the night's number and name, how many acts it has and how long, how many of the stage's doors open and from which acts, who comes and from which act, and what opens the next night ("Play this night, won or lost, and night 2 opens.", or "Night 2 is open."); at the right, under "NEW TONIGHT", the kinds of enemy that the night before did not have, each with its name large, its picture and one sentence of what it does; and at the top right what you have done on that night ("NOT PLAYED YET", your best act, or that you have won it). All of it is read from the night's numbers, so it is true of the show that follows. A night that is not open yet has a dark sheet with its name and what opens it, and nothing else. Left and right (the arrows, A and D, the d-pad or the stick) turn the pages, the shut ones too; Enter, Space or the gamepad's A or Start raise the curtain on an open night; Esc or Back goes back to the menu. When a show of that night was played since the game started, T (the gamepad's X) plays **the same show again**: the same seed, so the same enemies at the same doors on the same ticks, and the same cards in its encores. The seed is kept while the game runs, until F5 reads other numbers, and is written nowhere; a show played again is recorded in the progress as any other show is. The poster says nothing of light, house rules or an objective: the game has none yet.

**The nights and the progress.** A night is one performance, and `nights.json` (below) has three today: night 1 (five acts, critics alone, a smaller crowd), night 2 (seven acts, with the stagehand) and night 10, which is the game as `tuning.json` has it, ten acts. Night 1 is always open. Night 2 opens when night 1 has been played, whether it was won or lost, and night 10 when night 2 has been played. (The rule for the later nights is built too: from night 6 a night opens the next by reaching act five or by being won, and from night 16 by being won.) A show that ends is recorded, in its ovation or by the box office's fall; a show left by Esc or R is recorded as played once its first act is over, and one given up while its first act is played is not recorded at all. A show that is over says the two keys under the curtain ("R: the poster · Esc: the menu."), or "Night 2 is open. R: its poster · Esc: the menu." when it has just opened a night: R then goes to the new night's poster. A night that was played stays open.

The progress is kept in one small file, `progress.json`, in a folder named `The Understudies` in your own folder for applications' data: `~/Library/Application Support/The Understudies/` on macOS, `%LOCALAPPDATA%\The Understudies\` on Windows, `~/.local/share/The Understudies/` (or under `$XDG_DATA_HOME`) on Linux. The console says where when the game starts. **To start afresh, delete that file.** A file that is not the progress (damaged, empty, or of an older version) does not stop the game: it is renamed to `progress.json.damaged` and the game starts with no progress. A file that is there and that the game could not read or rename, or one written by a newer game, is left exactly as it is: the game then says so on the console, remembers the progress while it runs and writes nothing.

To play a night whatever the progress says, name it:

    dotnet run --project src/Understudies.Game -- --night 10

"Perform" then opens that night's poster, which has that page alone, and the progress file is neither read nor written.

**The spotlight night** is the first house rule (plan T56), and no night has it yet. To play it, put it on whatever night is played:

    dotnet run --project src/Understudies.Game -- --rule spotlight
    dotnet run --project src/Understudies.Game -- --rule spotlight --night 10

The stage is dark but for a circle round the magician (`spotlightRadius` in `tuning.json`, eight units) and a pool of light at every open door, falling from the door toward the box office. **What is dark is not drawn, and is still there**: a critic outside the light walks, strikes and is struck as ever, and is not seen until it comes into a lamp, where it comes out of the dark whole. What is seen whatever the light: every thrown card with its trail, the flick at a throwing hand, a strike's burst, The Burst's ring and a fall's splash and scraps, so the understudies' cards are what shows where the dark is not empty; applause, which glows; the box office's bar, the magician's pips, the footlights and every word. The set, the understudies and their routes are seen dimly through the dark, and the box office stands in a faint glow of its own that shows nobody: a critic that strikes it in the dark is not seen, and the box office is seen to flash. A fallen critic's body and a headliner's bar are hidden with their critic. At the ovation the lights come up. The poster of a night with the rule has a row "HOUSE RULES" with its name and its one sentence. A show played with `--rule` alone is remembered in the progress as any other (the rule only makes a night harder); with `--night` nothing is, as ever. A name that is no rule is refused on the console.

WASD, the arrows or a gamepad's left stick walk the magician. The dark band along the top of the stage is the back wall: the floor starts at its foot, and the magician cannot walk onto it. Critics come in by the lit doors, each standing whole in its lit frame when it is first seen (a door is three units of the stage's width, `stageDoorWidth`, and the two at the sides stand a door's half in from the edge), and nobody is pushed out of the stage's sides or up the back wall by a crowd, and walk to the box office to strike it. A critic the magician comes near turns on the magician instead, walks at it and hurts it by touching it; the moment the magician is out of its reach it goes back to the box office. From the fourth act on stagehands come in with the critics: smaller, rust where a critic is blue, four times as fast, and felled by one card. A stagehand never turns on the magician: it runs to the box office whoever stands in its way, and strikes it as a critic does. From the fourth act on the rival's understudies come in too: a cardboard cut-out of a rival magician on a brown board, as tall as the magician and nearly twice as wide. It is slower than a critic, never turns on the magician either, and each of its strikes takes three off the box office where a critic's takes one (`strikeDamage`, a number of each kind). It grows tougher by the act: ten hit points in the fourth act, where a critic has three, and four more in every act after it (`hitPointsPerAct`), thirty-four in the tenth. From the eighth act on the headliners come in as well, sixteen an act, spread over it, sometimes two or three at once and sometimes none for ten seconds: the same cut-out washed red, as slow and as wide, with a small red bar under its feet for what it has left: thirty hit points in the eighth act, forty-five in the ninth and sixty in the tenth, each strike taking three off the box office. A headliner hardly notices an understudy: one of their cards takes three in a hundred of its damage off it (`understudyDamageShare`, a number of each kind, 1 for the others), so by act ten one understudy's card is about a seventh of one of the magician's. Twelve throws of the magician's full hand fell one that stands alone in the eighth act and twenty-four in the tenth, and fewer in a crowd; nine understudies throwing at one headliner together take about as much off it as the magician does. Nothing blocks the magician: it walks through critics. The magician throws cards by itself, at the nearest enemy in range, and whoever has taken enough of them falls. A card is thrown ahead of a target that moves, at where the two will meet if the target goes on as it goes; it flies straight, so one that stops or turns while the card is in the air may be missed. Space, or a gamepad's A, is the Vanish: the magician is at once a few steps further the way it walks (the way it last walked, when it stands), for a moment nothing hurts it (it is pale and part see-through for as long), and where it stood it leaves a cloud that stuns the critics it touches: a stunned critic turns pale and neither walks nor hurts anything for a moment. Under the magician's feet a row of small green squares is what the magician has left, one for a hit point (eight, `magicianHitPoints`) and dark where one is lost, and the blue bar under the row fills as the Vanish comes back; a press before it is full does nothing. Many more hit points than eight are smaller squares, and more than fit are one bar. The bar above the box office, with the number beside it, is what the box office has left. When the box office's runs out the show closes and the stage goes dark. When the magician's runs out the magician falls, and the show goes on: it lies flat where it fell and takes no more keys for the rest of the act, no critic minds it any more, a line under the curtain says so, and the understudies play the act to its end. The next act starts with the magician whole and on its mark. The act it fell in was recorded only as far as the fall: in every later act that act's understudy plays up to that moment and is then gone from the stage.

A critic that falls to a card the magician itself threw leaves applause where it fell: a bright pink diamond, in a colour nothing else on the stage has. An understudy's card leaves none, whoever hurt the critic before it, and no card leaves any where the critic falls by the box office: nearer than eight units to its middle (`applauseBoxOfficeRadius`), on the floor that is a shade darker inside a thin broken line. What counts is where the critic falls, not where the magician stands: applause is earned by meeting the critics on their way in. The magician picks a piece up by coming near it (within `applausePickUpReach` of its own circle, which also takes a piece dropped by a critic that fell touching it), and nobody else can: an understudy walks over applause and leaves it, and so does nothing for a magician that has fallen. A piece lies for four seconds (`applauseTime`), dimming all the while, and is then gone. **The scalper eats it** (plan T57; `eatsApplause`, a kind's own flag): from the third act on a small quick figure with a fan of tickets for a head and a coat too long for it runs in by a door, six units a second, to the nearest piece of applause on the floor, wherever it lies (of two as near, the one dropped first), and eats the piece when its circle is on it, with a puff of torn tickets: the piece is gone and counts for nobody. Then it runs to the next, and with none on the floor it runs to the box office and strikes it as a critic does. It has one hit point, never turns on the magician, and leaves a piece of its own when the magician's card fells it outside the quiet floor, as anybody does. The magician picks up before anybody walks, so a piece both reach on one tick is the magician's; a stunned scalper eats nothing. Applause pays for encores. The pink bar in the middle of the curtain, under the words "applause to the next encore", is the way to the next one: the pieces picked up in this act that no encore was paid with, over what the next encore costs, with the two numbers beside its end. The first encore of a performance costs three pieces (`encoreFirstCost`) and every one taken makes the next cost one more (`encoreCostGrowth`), in whichever act it comes. Applause is not kept: going on to the next act clears the floor and empties the bar, and only the count of encores taken goes on.

When the bar is full the act stands for an encore: nothing moves, the act's time does not run, and three different self cards are offered on the dimmed stage, as the program's card is (below). The magician can hold three of a card (`cardMaxCopies`): an encore offers only cards it can still take, two or one when no more kinds are left, and when it holds three of all eight, the chorus card alone, taken as any encore's card is. The one taken is the magician's at once, its cost comes off the bar, what was over stays toward the next, and the act goes on. An encore waits a minute (`encoreTime`) and then takes its leftmost card. It opens in the middle of a fight, so for the first moment no card is taken, whatever is pressed. A piece picked up on an act's last tick opens none, and a magician that has fallen picks nothing up. Which cards an encore offers follows from the performance's seed and, once the magician holds three of a card, from the cards taken.

When an act in which at least one encore was taken is over and another follows, the program has one card: the chorus card. An act without an encore has no program, and the line under the curtain says so: the chorus grows only by the magician's applause. The program's card is shown as an encore's three cards are: the stage stands and is dimmed by six tenths, with the critics left on it still to be made out, and the offer is paper cards side by side across the middle of the floor, each in an ink border with a hard shadow. A card has a head strip that says whose it is, gold for the magician and green for every understudy, with its key at the strip's left end; under it the card's stage name, a line of flavour in a smaller face, which says nothing of the rule, and the sentence that does: what one more of it changes, in the numbers of `tuning.json` ("Each copy again." is left off the copy that would be the last the limit allows); and at its foot how many of it are held, for a self card of how many it may hold ("you have 2 of 3", "you have none yet"). The highlighted card stands a little higher than the others in a ring, pink for an encore's self cards and green for the chorus card, and it is the leftmost when the offer opens. Over the cards is a row of what is held so far, a paper chip for each card with its name and how many of it, for a self card of how many it may hold ("2/3"; a card that is full is a gold chip, and the chorus card's is pale green and says "×4"); with nothing held the row's place says so. The left and right arrows, A and D, a gamepad's d-pad or its left stick move the highlight, and Enter, Space or the gamepad's A take the highlighted card; the keys 1, 2 and 3 take the card in that place at once. Only a press counts: a key or a stick held since the act (Space for a Vanish, D to walk) does nothing until it has been let go. Under the cards the program's minute (`programTime`), or an encore's (`encoreTime`), runs down as a number and as a bar, with the words for what its end does: the leftmost card is taken; and under that the keys, each drawn as a key cap. While an encore is read the clock on the curtain is grey over the words "the clock stands", and when an act is over it is grey over "the stage stands", with "is over" under the act's number. The box office's bar is not drawn while the stage stands for a screen, an encore's or an act's end, and the magician's pips are dimmed with the stage. The program's card, taken by a key or by the clock, stays lit for most of a second with a chime, the line under the curtain naming it, and in that moment Enter does not go on; then the stage is between two acts. An encore's card is taken with the same chime and the act goes on at once. In an encore a second line under the curtain says how many encores were taken so far and what this one was earned with. What is held so far in this performance is a row of paper chips under the words "you hold", a card's name and how many of it on each, the chorus card's pale green: in an act at the left above the footlights, and while the stage stands, for an encore, the program or between two acts, in the row over the cards or over their place. There are nine cards, by the names of the card catalogue (`docs/design/2026-10-08-card-catalogue.md`), and any of them may be taken again: a self card until three of it are held (the plan's decision 31), the chorus card without end. Eight are self cards, which an encore offers, three different ones each time while that many are left to take, and which change the magician from then on. Five add a number: Card Sharp (a thrown card takes `cardDamage`, half a hit point, more), Sleight of Hand (throws come `cardAttackSpeed`, a fifth, more often: the time between two is the `throwCooldown` over 1.2, and over 1.4 with two), Long Arm (the throw reaches `cardRange`, a unit and a half, further, and its cards fly that far), Quick Smoke (the Vanish comes back `cardVanishCooldown`, a quarter, faster, counted the same way: its wait is the `vanishCooldown` over 1.25, a fifth shorter) and One More for the Lady (each throw sends one more, at the next nearest enemy in range: with one enemy in range, one card). Three change what a thrown card does when it strikes. The Pierce: the card goes on through the enemy it strikes, and each strike takes `cardPierceLoss`, half a hit point, off what it hurts for, until nothing is left or its range is flown; with two of the card the loss is half of that, with three a third, and a card strikes nobody twice. The Ricochet: a card that strikes turns to the nearest enemy it has not struck within `cardRicochetReach`, four units, of the one it struck, once for each card of the name; the turn takes nothing off it, and it flies as far as that enemy at least. The Burst: every other enemy within `cardBurstRadius`, two units, of the one a card struck takes `cardBurstShare`, half, of what the card hurt for, and the whole of it with two of the card. A card that has a turn left turns before it goes through anybody; a burst comes with every strike, a later one with what the card hurts for by then; each card of a throw of several does all of it; and an enemy that falls to the magician's burst leaves applause as one that falls to its card does. An understudy is the magician of the act it was recorded in, tick for tick: it begins every act with the self cards the magician had when that act began, and gains each card of that act's encores at the moment of the act the magician took it, with a puff of scraps. A card taken in a later act changes no understudy of an act that was over. The Vanish cooldown card does nothing for an understudy, whose Vanishes are where the magician's were. The ninth is the chorus card, the program's, and an encore's once the magician holds three of every self card: every understudy's cards, those there are and those there will be, take `cardChorusDamage`, half a hit point, more, on top of what each has of its own, and the magician's own cards do not.

When an act is over and another follows, four dark panels in a cream border stand on the dimmed stage, two at each side of the middle, from the act's last tick until the next act is gone on to: in the program with the chorus card between them, and between two acts, after the card is taken or when the act had no encore, with the stage's middle between them and under it the one key there is, "Enter or Start raises the curtain". The side doors are clear of them, so the door the next act opens by is seen. **The act on the books** (top left): the applause picked up in the act, the encores taken in it and tonight, how many critics the magician's own cards felled, what the box office has left and what it lost in this act, and whether the magician stood to the end or fell. **The understudy that joins** (bottom left): the magician's figure in the colour of the act, as it will stand on the stage, with what it does from now on (it runs this route in every act to come, or to where the magician fell) and the self cards it holds at its act's end, which are the magician's own. **What the next act brings** (top right): how many enter and of which kinds, by the kinds' names in `tuning.json`; whether a door opens, and which, with how many are open; and what the next encore costs. **The cast at the curtain** (bottom right): a square for every understudy with its act's number on its act's colour, the one that joins among them, and under each how many critics its cards felled in the act just over: what each of the magician's past selves did, after every act. Every number there is the simulation's own; the game keeps two of them itself while an act runs, what the box office had when the act began and whose card each fall was by, as the simulation reported it.

A blow is shown as well as counted. A critic that a card hurts flashes white; one that falls bursts into scraps of paper and lies where it fell, fading. A thrown card spins and trails a streak, with a flick of light at the hand that threw it, smaller and dimmer at an understudy's hand than at the magician's; where it strikes there is a burst of its suits, and where its critic falls a splash of ink and newsprint over a pale flash. A card with The Pierce does not stop, and leaves a line of those bursts; one with The Ricochet starts its streak again where it turned; and The Burst is a pale ring that opens round the enemy the card struck, as wide as the rule's radius, dimmer for an understudy's card. The magician flashes when a touch hurts it, the box office when it is struck, and the whole stage shakes with either: a little for one blow, more under a crowd's, and no more than that however big the crowd. A Vanish holds everything still for a moment and puffs scraps where the magician left and where it arrives, and a piece of applause that is picked up puffs a few where it lay. None of this is a rule, so its numbers are not in `tuning.json`: they are the constants at the top of `src/Understudies.Game/Juice.cs`, and a changed one needs the game started again.

A blow is heard too. Seven short sounds are worked out in code when the game starts, and there is no sound file: a flick for a thrown card, a dry tap for a card that hurts a critic, a falling tone with paper tearing in it for a critic that falls, a whoosh for the Vanish, a low thump for a strike on the box office, a buzz when a critic hurts the magician, and a short bright chime for a piece of applause picked up (one that is dropped makes no sound, and neither does one a scalper eats: its puff of tickets is all that says so), which is heard for a card taken from an encore or from the program too. A sound comes a little from the side of the stage it happened on, is a little higher or lower each time, and does not start again within a twentieth of a second of its own last start, however many blows a crowd deals in that time. A throw, a hit and a kill may start twice in that time, an understudy's and then the magician's own. What an understudy does (its throw, its hit, its kill) is the same sound at half the loudness and a little lower, so the past is heard apart from the present, and it never holds back the magician's own. M mutes the sound and M again brings it back; a sound that has started rings out. A machine with no audio device runs the game silent and says so on the console. The numbers of the sound (how loud each is, the gap between two starts, every recipe) are the constants at the top of `src/Understudies.Game/Sound.cs`, and a changed one needs the game started again.

A performance is ten acts of seventy-five seconds (`actsInPerformance` and `actLength` in `tuning.json`), or as many acts as its night has: five on night 1 and seven on night 2. The curtain along the back wall says which act it is, at the left in gold, and how much of its time is left, at the right, with how many encores were taken tonight under the clock; at the right above the footlights the cast is a row of squares, one for each understudy with its act's number on its act's colour (dimmed when that understudy has left the stage) and a dark one with a broken border for the act that is played and recorded; when the act is over the cast is on its panel instead. An act ends when its time runs out, whatever is on the stage. The stage then stands, while what was in the air settles: first for the program, if the act had an encore, until its card is taken, and then until Enter, or a gamepad's Start, goes on to the next act: the magician begins it whole and on its mark, and the critics of the last act are still where they stood. Every act, the first too, begins with the curtain: one second (`curtainTime`) in which the stage stands, the act's time does not run and no key is taken, a press of the Vanish included. In that second every understudy slides back along its own route, from where it stood when the last act ended to where its route starts, fast at first and settling; and through the curtain of the second act and the first three seconds of that act, and never again in that performance, one line over the first understudy's head, going where it goes, says what it is. Every act that was played leaves an understudy, from the next act on: the magician as a cardboard figure in the colour of its act, with the act's number over its head in that colour, and on the floor a dashed line of where it goes in the next two and a half seconds, fading with how far ahead that is, with a gap where it vanishes (plan T43). While the stage stands between two acts, in the program and under the curtain, the line is the first two and a half seconds of each route from the mark: where everybody goes when the next act begins. The whole route is not drawn: nine of them were a tangle. Understudies that stand on one spot are drawn the newest in front, with their numbers side by side; the magician is drawn over an understudy that stands where it does, and an understudy is the fainter the nearer it is to the magician's spot, with its number beside the others' over the head. At the back door, where there is no floor over a head, the number is under the feet. The first understudy, while its caption is up, has the caption for its label and is drawn whole and over the magician. It stands, tick for tick, where the magician stood in that act, leaves a cloud where and when the magician vanished, and throws cards (a little duller than the magician's own) at whatever critic is in its range now. Nothing hurts, pushes or stops it, and the critics take no notice of it. After the last act the performance ends in a standing ovation, and the line under the curtain names the two keys. While a show is played R starts a new performance of the same night at once; when it is over, in its ovation or by the box office's fall, R, or the gamepad's Y, goes to the poster, of the night the show has just opened or else of the night that was played. Esc, or the gamepad's Back, goes back to the main menu.

Who enters when is planned for the whole performance when it starts, from its seed. Every act has a budget (`firstActBudget`, `budgetGrowthPerAct` more in the second act, and that step bigger by `budgetGrowthRise` in every act after it) and buys enemies with it from `enemyKinds`, each kind at its `cost`, by its `weight` among the kinds the act may have (`fromAct`) and can still afford, until it can afford none. There are five kinds, the critic, from the third act the scalper (plan T57: the last in the file's list, where a kind is added so that no other's place moves), from the fourth the stagehand and the rival's understudy, and from the eighth the headliner, each at a cost of 1, and a critic is bought three times as readily as a scalper, a stagehand or a rival's understudy: the first act has forty critics, the second eighty, the third a hundred and eighty enemies, one in four of them a scalper, and the tenth two thousand five hundred and sixty, about one in six of them a scalper, one in six a stagehand and one in six a rival's understudy. The headliner is not drawn for (its `weight` is 0): every act from the eighth has sixteen (`inAnAct`), paid for from the budget before anything is drawn and set one to each sixteenth of the buying order, which is not one to each sixteenth of the act's time: the crowds (below) move every fourth entry, headliners among them, so two or three may enter on one tick. A kind that an act may not have yet takes no part in that act's draws, so the acts before the eighth are planned as they were before there was a headliner. An act's critics are spread evenly over it, each a little earlier or later by chance, but a quarter of them (`waveBurstShare`) do not wait for their own moment: every ten seconds (`waveBurstTime`) a crowd comes in together, by whichever doors its critics were given. Nobody enters in its last seconds (`actQuietEnd`) or while its curtain is up. Each enters by one of the doors that are open in that act: a stage door opens in the act `opensInAct` names and stays open, the first in act one, the second in act three and the third in act six. An open door is lit and a shut one dim, from the act's curtain on, and the curtain says, under the act's number, how many critics are still to come in this act.

The words are drawn in two faces whose files are in the repository, so every machine shows the same: Atkinson Hyperlegible for every sentence and every number (the act, the clock, the counts, how many of a card are held, a square of the cast), and Pixelify Sans Bold for a card's name on its card, the main menu's title and entries, and on the poster the night's name and a new kind's, and for nothing else. A card's head strip, the HUD's small labels in capitals and a card's name on a chip are Atkinson Hyperlegible too: the design has them in Pixelify Sans, whose capital C was read as an O there. Words on the stage carry a dark outline; words on a card's paper have none. The files are in `fonts/`, as their authors distribute them and unmodified: `fonts/pixelify-sans/` (Copyright 2021 The Pixelify Sans Project Authors) and `fonts/atkinson-hyperlegible/` (Copyright 2020 Braille Institute of America, Inc.), each under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, whose text is the `OFL.txt` beside them. The game reads the `fonts` folder of the directory it is started from if it has every file the game needs, and otherwise the copy beside the executable (the build puts one there, licence texts included); when a file is missing from both it names the file on the console and does not start.

Every number of the rules is in `tuning.json` at the repository root. The game reads the `tuning.json` of the directory it is started from, so run it from the root, and the copy beside the executable when there is none. Edit the file and press F5 (fn+F5 on a Mac's built-in keyboard): the game reads it and `nights.json` again and goes on with the new numbers, as the night that is played has them. What an act starts with (its length, its curtain's, the magician's mark and hit points) counts from the next act, and the box office's hit points from the next performance, so press R as well; a countdown that is already running (the curtain's time, the act's time, a critic's next blow, the next throw, the Vanish coming back, a cloud's time, a stun, a piece of applause's time, an encore's time, the program's time) runs out at its old length first. A critic already on the stage keeps the hit points it entered with, and a card already in the air the range and the damage it was thrown with, and with them what a strike takes off it, the share of its burst and how often it may turn; the ricochet's reach and the burst's radius count at once. What a card gives counts at once for every card of that name already taken, the understudies' too: the magician and each understudy keep how many of each card they have, and not the numbers that came of them. The waves are not planned again: who enters when, by which door and of which kind was settled when the performance started, so new budgets, kinds' costs and weights and `actQuietEnd` count from the next performance (R), and so do doors' acts for who enters where. A planned entry names its door and its kind by their places in `stageDoors` and `enemyKinds`, so those lists are not to be reordered or shortened in a performance under way. Three more things a reload does to a performance under way: more `actsInPerformance` gives the added acts no critics; a shorter `actLength` leaves out the entries planned past the act's new end; and a door's new `opensInAct` lights or dims the door at once, while the critics still enter by the doors they were planned for. A key the game does not know or a key that is missing is an error that names the key, and so is a stage door above `stageFloorTop`, the foot of the back wall (the third door stands at that foot, so the two numbers change together), no door open in act one, no kind of enemy, and a kind whose `cost` is less than 1; the console shows it, and the game keeps the numbers it had (or, at start-up, does not start).

`nights.json`, beside it, is the first of the ladder of nights (the plan's "The ladder"): a list of nights, each its number, its `name` on its poster (which changes nothing, and may be left out: the poster then has "Night 3" for a name) and what it changes of `tuning.json` for that night: the number of acts, a scale for the acts' budgets, the kinds of enemy allowed, the crowds; and its house rules, by their names (`"rules": ["spotlight"]`: the one rule there is, a name that is not a rule is refused, and no committed night has one). Night 1 is five acts of critics alone at six tenths of the budget with no crowds, night 2 seven acts with the stagehand at seven tenths, and night 10 changes nothing. The game plays the night chosen on its menu (above) and finds the file as it finds `tuning.json`; a file with no night in it, or a night that names a kind the tuning does not have, is an error on the console as a bad `tuning.json` is. F5 reads it again: in a show the entries stay as they were planned, so a night's new scale or kinds count from the next show (R) and its number of acts at once; and when the file no longer has the night that is played, the numbers stay as they were.

To look at a frame without playing, the game can play by itself for a number of ticks (60 a second), save the frame it ends on and exit. It is played by the doors player of the guard (the same file the tests use): it goes out to the newest door, picks up its applause and takes every encore's card and the program's by the players' one order. The capture's seed is one the guard plays, and on the committed numbers its performance reaches the ovation; the guard lets four seeds of twenty lose, so if a retuning makes this one lose, the capture is given a seed that wins. A card takes none of a capture's ticks, and the next act is gone on to at once; the ticks of every act's curtain are counted with the rest, sixty before each act, so on the committed numbers act n begins on tick 4560 × (n − 1).

A capture is silent: it makes no sound and opens no audio device.

The window opens at 1280 × 720 and can be resized; the stage keeps its shape in it. The sprites are drawn to measures in the stage's units, whatever the window (plan T42). The set (the curtain with a gold line under it, the doors, the box office, the footlights) is as large as its units say: the curtain three, the box office four, a door three. The walking figures (the magician and its understudies, the critic, the stagehand, the rival's understudy, the headliner, which is the rival's picture with a red wash, and the scalper), their fallen bodies and the two bursts are `FiguresMeasure` times the set's measure. At the committed 1 the magician is 3.09 units tall, as it was in the window of 1024 the game used to open in; at the design's 1.6 it is 4.95, on a stage of 27. A sprite pixel is so a whole number of screen pixels only where the window makes it one: at 1280 wide the set's and a figure's are one and a quarter (every fourth is two screen pixels, seen when looked for), and at 1.6 a figure's is two. The boards alone are laid at a whole number, two screen pixels at 1280. A figure stands taller and wider than its footprint, and no footprint, range or radius follows the measure. The footlights are whole in an act, at half while an encore is read and at 0.35 in the program and between two acts.

The figures, the stage set and the two bursts of a card's effects are pixel-art sprites made with ludo.ai, read from `art/ludo/sprites/` (the repository's own when the game is run from its root, a copy beside the executable otherwise). Each of the nine cards has a picture of what it does on its card, where a card is chosen: `art/ludo/cards/`, a file named by the card's name in code, and the candidates they were picked from under `art/ludo/cards/candidates/`. `art/prompt-guide.md` has the recipe they were made by, and each `manifest.json` under `art/ludo/` the prompt and the settings of every generation.

## Tests

    dotnet test Understudies.sln --configuration Release

Three scripted players measure whether hiding at the box office loses and going out to the doors wins. The orbit player walks round the box office and never leaves its circle; it is played on three circles (on the box office, close round it, and five units out), and hiding has lost on a seed only when it lost on all three. The doors player holds the door that opened last, standing at it (a throw's range in, and no more than a third of the way to the box office), fetches applause up to its door and never into the doorway, steps back from a critic that is upon it, and goes back to the box office while a critic is at it. The kiter is the rival of going out: it walks round the box office just outside the floor where no fall earns applause, dodges as the doors player does, and steps out to a piece within nine units of its place on the circle, but to none that lies nearer an open door than the doors player's post there. All take an encore's card by one fixed order, and the program's card when there is one. Ordinary tests hold the committed `tuning.json` to the guard of the plan's decision 22 on two sets of seeds, 1 to 20, which the numbers were tuned on, and 101 to 120: the orbit player has to lose the box office by the end of act six, and the doors player to finish act ten, in at least 16 of the first set and 14 of the second; and the doors player has to do better than the kiter (finish at least as often, with half as many encores again and fifty more of the box office left). And two tripwires on the first set (plan T29): the doors player's box office at the end is at least 350 of 400 on average, and taking its cards by another order, the longer arm first, it still finishes on at least 16 of 20. On the committed numbers the doors player finishes on every seed with some 35 encores and 366 of the box office, struck in the last three acts, when the headliners come, and its own card strikes the last blow on some two headliners in three (how much of a headliner's hit points the magician takes is not measured); the kiter finishes every seed too, with 20 encores and 295 of the box office (21 and 300 on the second set). The scalpers eat a tenth of what the doors player drops in the third act and two fifths in the tenth, and a third to three fifths of what the kiter drops, and cost neither an encore: they eat what was left lying (plan T57). So hiding on the ring does not lose, which nothing asserts: whether it must is the owner's open question. The order the cards are taken by decides little now, since nobody holds more than three of a card (plan T41), every player has the same hand from about act six, and a headliner is hurt by the magician's hand and hardly by an understudy's (plan T46): with the longer arm first the doors player finishes 20 of 20 with 356 of the box office, with "one more card" last 20 with 322, and by the weakest order of the instrument 20 with 309. The players' order has the three cards that change what a card does after "one more card", the damage card and the attack speed card and ahead of the longer arm and the quicker Vanish. The plan's T37, T29, T25, T41, T46 and T57 have the tables, the open question and what the guard measures and what it does not. This prints the performances of the first set in full, how every one ended, each act's averages, its applause and its encores among them, and the guard's counts (it takes a few seconds, and asserts nothing):

    dotnet test tests/Understudies.Core.Tests --configuration Release --filter "FullyQualifiedName~ScriptedPlayersTests.PrintTheGuardsTable" --logger "console;verbosity=detailed"

For another budget, or any other variant of the tuning, there is `PrintTheVariants`, which plays the seeds 1 to 20 alone unless `UNDERSTUDIES_SEED_SETS` names other sets by their first seeds (`1,101`): numbers are searched on the first set only (the plan's seeds' rule says which set is read once on a candidate and which only for the last check). `PrintTheCardOrders` plays the doors player and the kiter by eleven orders of taking cards: the committed one, its reverse, "one more card" last, the longer arm first, each of the three new cards first, each of them in the place of "one more card", and the three new cards last, as they are and made to do nothing.

Two more of the ordinary tests pin the state hash of a scripted performance: the same seed and the same player must end in the same state on macOS ARM and on Linux x64, and CI runs on both. A change to `tuning.json`, to a rule or to a player changes those hashes, and the tests' numbers are pinned again.
