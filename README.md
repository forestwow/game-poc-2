# The Understudies

A prototype of a short roguelite for PC. You are a stage magician defending the box office from critics. Each act lasts about a minute, and every act you play leaves behind a cardboard understudy that repeats the route you ran, in every act that follows. Where you run is what you build.

- `docs/vision.md` is the design.
- `docs/plan-prototype.md` is the plan of the prototype and its ticket list.

## Layout

- `src/Understudies.Core`: the rules of the game, with no engine dependency.
- `src/Understudies.Game`: the MonoGame layer (window, input, drawing, sound).
- `tests/Understudies.Core.Tests`: NUnit tests for Core.

## Requirements

The .NET SDK named in `global.json` (10.0.1xx). Nothing else: MonoGame and the text library come from NuGet, and the fonts are in the repository.

## Running

    dotnet run --project src/Understudies.Game

WASD, the arrows or a gamepad's left stick walk the magician. The dark band along the top of the stage is the back wall: the floor starts at its foot, and the magician cannot walk onto it. Critics come in by the lit doors and walk to the box office to strike it. A critic the magician comes near turns on the magician instead, walks at it and hurts it by touching it; the moment the magician is out of its reach it goes back to the box office. From the fourth act on stagehands come in with the critics: smaller, rust where a critic is blue, four times as fast, and felled by one card. A stagehand never turns on the magician: it runs to the box office whoever stands in its way, and strikes it as a critic does. From the fourth act on the rival's understudies come in too: a cardboard cut-out of a rival magician on a brown board, as tall as the magician and nearly twice as wide. It is slower than a critic, never turns on the magician either, and each of its strikes takes three off the box office where a critic's takes one (`strikeDamage`, a number of each kind). It grows tougher by the act: ten hit points in the fourth act, where a critic has three, and four more in every act after it (`hitPointsPerAct`), thirty-four in the tenth. Nothing blocks the magician: it walks through critics. The magician throws cards by itself, at the nearest enemy in range, and whoever has taken enough of them falls. A card is thrown ahead of a target that moves, at where the two will meet if the target goes on as it goes; it flies straight, so one that stops or turns while the card is in the air may be missed. Space, or a gamepad's A, is the Vanish: the magician is at once a few steps further the way it walks (the way it last walked, when it stands), for a moment nothing hurts it (it is pale and part see-through for as long), and where it stood it leaves a cloud that stuns the critics it touches: a stunned critic turns pale and neither walks nor hurts anything for a moment. The small bar over the magician's head fills as the Vanish comes back; a press before it is full does nothing. The small bar under the magician's feet is what the magician has left, and the bar above the box office, with the number beside it, what the box office has left. When the box office's runs out the show closes and the stage goes dark. When the magician's runs out the magician falls, and the show goes on: it lies flat where it fell and takes no more keys for the rest of the act, no critic minds it any more, a line on the back wall says so, and the understudies play the act to its end. The next act starts with the magician whole and on its mark. The act it fell in was recorded only as far as the fall: in every later act that act's understudy plays up to that moment and is then gone from the stage.

A critic that falls to a card the magician itself threw leaves applause where it fell: a bright pink diamond, in a colour nothing else on the stage has. An understudy's card leaves none, whoever hurt the critic before it, and no card leaves any where the critic falls by the box office: nearer than eight units to its middle (`applauseBoxOfficeRadius`), on the floor that is a shade darker inside a thin broken line. What counts is where the critic falls, not where the magician stands: applause is earned by meeting the critics on their way in. The magician picks a piece up by coming near it (within `applausePickUpReach` of its own circle, which also takes a piece dropped by a critic that fell touching it), and nobody else can: an understudy walks over applause and leaves it, and so does nothing for a magician that has fallen. A piece lies for four seconds (`applauseTime`), dimming all the while, and is then gone. Applause pays for encores. The pink bar at the foot of the back wall is the way to the next one: the pieces picked up in this act that no encore was paid with, over what the next encore costs, with the two numbers beside its end. The first encore of a performance costs three pieces (`encoreFirstCost`) and every one taken makes the next cost one more (`encoreCostGrowth`), in whichever act it comes. Applause is not kept: going on to the next act clears the floor and empties the bar, and only the count of encores taken goes on.

When the bar is full the act stands for an encore: nothing moves, the act's time does not run, and three different self cards are offered on the dimmed stage, as the program's card is (below). The one taken is the magician's at once, its cost comes off the bar, what was over stays toward the next, and the act goes on. An encore waits six seconds (`encoreTime`) and then takes its leftmost card. It opens in the middle of a fight, so for the first moment no card is taken, whatever is pressed. A piece picked up on an act's last tick opens none, and a magician that has fallen picks nothing up. Which cards an encore offers follows from the performance's seed.

When an act in which at least one encore was taken is over and another follows, the program has one card: the chorus card. An act without an encore has no program, and the back wall says so: the chorus grows only by the magician's applause. The program is a screen of its own, and an encore's three cards are shown the same way: the stage stands and is dimmed a little, with the critics left on it still seen, and the offer is paper cards side by side across the front of the floor. Each card has a band along its top that says whose it is, yellow for the magician and green for every understudy, with its place at the band's left end and, at its right end, how many of it are already held; under it the card's stage name, a line of flavour in a smaller face, which says nothing of the rule, and the sentence that does: what one more of it changes, in the numbers of `tuning.json`. The highlighted card is the bright one in a white frame, a little higher than the others, and it is the leftmost when the offer opens. The left and right arrows, A and D, a gamepad's d-pad or its left stick move the highlight, and Enter, Space or the gamepad's A take the highlighted card; the keys 1, 2 and 3 take the card in that place at once. Only a press counts: a key or a stick held since the act (Space for a Vanish, D to walk) does nothing until it has been let go. Under the cards the program's twelve seconds (`programTime`), or an encore's six, run down as a number and as a bar, with the words for what the end of them does: the leftmost card is taken. The program's card, taken by a key or by the clock, stays lit for most of a second with a chime, the back wall naming it, and in that moment Enter does not go on; then the stage is between two acts. An encore's card is taken with the same chime and the act goes on at once. In an encore, in the program and between two acts a line under the applause bar says how many encores were taken so far and what the next costs, and a line at the stage's bottom edge, or two, what is held so far in this performance. There are nine cards, by the names of the card catalogue (`docs/design/2026-10-08-card-catalogue.md`), and any of them may be taken again, with no limit on copies. Eight are self cards, which an encore offers, three different ones each time, and which change the magician from then on. Five add a number: Card Sharp (a thrown card takes `cardDamage`, half a hit point, more), Sleight of Hand (throws come `cardAttackSpeed`, a fifth, more often: the time between two is the `throwCooldown` over 1.2, and over 1.4 with two), Long Arm (the throw reaches `cardRange`, a unit and a half, further, and its cards fly that far), Quick Smoke (the Vanish comes back `cardVanishCooldown`, a quarter, faster, counted the same way: its wait is the `vanishCooldown` over 1.25, a fifth shorter) and One More for the Lady (each throw sends one more, at the next nearest enemy in range: with one enemy in range, one card). Three change what a thrown card does when it strikes. The Pierce: the card goes on through the enemy it strikes, and each strike takes `cardPierceLoss`, half a hit point, off what it hurts for, until nothing is left or its range is flown; with two of the card the loss is half of that, with three a third, and a card strikes nobody twice. The Ricochet: a card that strikes turns to the nearest enemy it has not struck within `cardRicochetReach`, four units, of the one it struck, once for each card of the name; the turn takes nothing off it, and it flies as far as that enemy at least. The Burst: every other enemy within `cardBurstRadius`, two units, of the one a card struck takes `cardBurstShare`, half, of what the card hurt for, and the whole of it with two of the card. A card that has a turn left turns before it goes through anybody; a burst comes with every strike, a later one with what the card hurts for by then; each card of a throw of several does all of it; and an enemy that falls to the magician's burst leaves applause as one that falls to its card does. An understudy is the magician of the act it was recorded in, tick for tick: it begins every act with the self cards the magician had when that act began, and gains each card of that act's encores at the moment of the act the magician took it, with a puff of scraps. A card taken in a later act changes no understudy of an act that was over. The Vanish cooldown card does nothing for an understudy, whose Vanishes are where the magician's were. The sixth is the chorus card, the program's: every understudy's cards, those there are and those there will be, take `cardChorusDamage`, half a hit point, more, on top of what each has of its own, and the magician's own cards do not.

A blow is shown as well as counted. A critic that a card hurts flashes white; one that falls bursts into scraps of paper and lies where it fell, fading. A thrown card spins and trails a streak, with a flick of light at the hand that threw it, smaller and dimmer at an understudy's hand than at the magician's; where it strikes there is a burst of its suits, and where its critic falls a splash of ink and newsprint over a pale flash. A card with The Pierce does not stop, and leaves a line of those bursts; one with The Ricochet starts its streak again where it turned; and The Burst is a pale ring that opens round the enemy the card struck, as wide as the rule's radius, dimmer for an understudy's card. The magician flashes when a touch hurts it, the box office when it is struck, and the whole stage shakes with either: a little for one blow, more under a crowd's, and no more than that however big the crowd. A Vanish holds everything still for a moment and puffs scraps where the magician left and where it arrives, and a piece of applause that is picked up puffs a few where it lay. None of this is a rule, so its numbers are not in `tuning.json`: they are the constants at the top of `src/Understudies.Game/Juice.cs`, and a changed one needs the game started again.

A blow is heard too. Seven short sounds are worked out in code when the game starts, and there is no sound file: a flick for a thrown card, a dry tap for a card that hurts a critic, a falling tone with paper tearing in it for a critic that falls, a whoosh for the Vanish, a low thump for a strike on the box office, a buzz when a critic hurts the magician, and a short bright chime for a piece of applause picked up (one that is dropped makes no sound), which is heard for a card taken from an encore or from the program too. A sound comes a little from the side of the stage it happened on, is a little higher or lower each time, and does not start again within a twentieth of a second of its own last start, however many blows a crowd deals in that time. A throw, a hit and a kill may start twice in that time, an understudy's and then the magician's own. What an understudy does (its throw, its hit, its kill) is the same sound at half the loudness and a little lower, so the past is heard apart from the present, and it never holds back the magician's own. M mutes the sound and M again brings it back; a sound that has started rings out. A machine with no audio device runs the game silent and says so on the console. The numbers of the sound (how loud each is, the gap between two starts, every recipe) are the constants at the top of `src/Understudies.Game/Sound.cs`, and a changed one needs the game started again.

A performance is ten acts of seventy-five seconds (`actsInPerformance` and `actLength` in `tuning.json`); the back wall says which act it is and how much of its time is left. An act ends when its time runs out, whatever is on the stage. The stage then stands, while what was in the air settles: first for the program, if the act had an encore, until its card is taken, and then until Enter, or a gamepad's Start, goes on to the next act: the magician begins it whole and on its mark, and the critics of the last act are still where they stood. Every act, the first too, begins with the curtain: one second (`curtainTime`) in which the stage stands, the act's time does not run and no key is taken, a press of the Vanish included. In that second every understudy slides back along its own route, from where it stood when the last act ended to where its route starts, fast at first and settling; and through the curtain of the second act and the first three seconds of that act, and never again in that performance, one line over the first understudy's head, going where it goes, says what it is. Every act that was played leaves an understudy, from the next act on: a pale, half-transparent copy of the magician in the colour of its act, with the line of its route faint on the floor. It stands, tick for tick, where the magician stood in that act, leaves a cloud where and when the magician vanished, and throws cards (a little duller than the magician's own) at whatever critic is in its range now. Nothing hurts, pushes or stops it, and the critics take no notice of it. After the last act the performance ends in a standing ovation. R starts a new performance, at any time. Esc quits.

Who enters when is planned for the whole performance when it starts, from its seed. Every act has a budget (`firstActBudget`, `budgetGrowthPerAct` more in the second act, and that step bigger by `budgetGrowthRise` in every act after it) and buys enemies with it from `enemyKinds`, each kind at its `cost`, by its `weight` among the kinds the act may have (`fromAct`) and can still afford, until it can afford none. There are three kinds, the critic and, from the fourth act, the stagehand and the rival's understudy, each at a cost of 1, and a critic is bought three times as readily as either of the others: the first act has forty critics, the second eighty, the third a hundred and eighty, and the tenth two thousand five hundred and sixty enemies, about one in five of them a stagehand and one in five a rival's understudy. An act's critics are spread evenly over it, each a little earlier or later by chance, but a quarter of them (`waveBurstShare`) do not wait for their own moment: every ten seconds (`waveBurstTime`) a crowd comes in together, by whichever doors its critics were given. Nobody enters in its last seconds (`actQuietEnd`) or while its curtain is up. Each enters by one of the doors that are open in that act: a stage door opens in the act `opensInAct` names and stays open, the first in act one, the second in act three and the third in act six. An open door is lit and a shut one dim, from the act's curtain on, and the back wall says, beside the act's number, how many critics are still to come in this act.

The words are drawn in two faces whose files are in the repository, so every machine shows the same: Pixelify Sans for a heading, a name, a number and a label (Bold for the act, the clock and a card's name, SemiBold for the rest) and Atkinson Hyperlegible for a sentence. Words on the stage carry a dark outline; words on a card's paper have none. The files are in `fonts/`, as their authors distribute them and unmodified: `fonts/pixelify-sans/` (Copyright 2021 The Pixelify Sans Project Authors) and `fonts/atkinson-hyperlegible/` (Copyright 2020 Braille Institute of America, Inc.), each under the SIL Open Font License 1.1, whose text is the `OFL.txt` beside them. The game reads the `fonts` folder of the directory it is started from and the copy beside the executable when there is none (the build puts one there, licence texts included), and without either it says so on the console and does not start.

Every number of the rules is in `tuning.json` at the repository root. The game reads the `tuning.json` of the directory it is started from, so run it from the root, and the copy beside the executable when there is none. Edit the file and press F5 (fn+F5 on a Mac's built-in keyboard): the game goes on with the new numbers. What an act starts with (its length, its curtain's, the magician's mark and hit points) counts from the next act, and the box office's hit points from the next performance, so press R as well; a countdown that is already running (the curtain's time, the act's time, a critic's next blow, the next throw, the Vanish coming back, a cloud's time, a stun, a piece of applause's time, an encore's time, the program's time) runs out at its old length first. A critic already on the stage keeps the hit points it entered with, and a card already in the air the range and the damage it was thrown with, and with them what a strike takes off it, the share of its burst and how often it may turn; the ricochet's reach and the burst's radius count at once. What a card gives counts at once for every card of that name already taken, the understudies' too: the magician and each understudy keep how many of each card they have, and not the numbers that came of them. The waves are not planned again: who enters when, by which door and of which kind was settled when the performance started, so new budgets, kinds' costs and weights and `actQuietEnd` count from the next performance (R), and so do doors' acts for who enters where. A planned entry names its door and its kind by their places in `stageDoors` and `enemyKinds`, so those lists are not to be reordered or shortened in a performance under way. Three more things a reload does to a performance under way: more `actsInPerformance` gives the added acts no critics; a shorter `actLength` leaves out the entries planned past the act's new end; and a door's new `opensInAct` lights or dims the door at once, while the critics still enter by the doors they were planned for. A key the game does not know or a key that is missing is an error that names the key, and so is a stage door above `stageFloorTop`, the foot of the back wall (the third door stands at that foot, so the two numbers change together), no door open in act one, no kind of enemy, and a kind whose `cost` is less than 1; the console shows it, and the game keeps the numbers it had (or, at start-up, does not start).

To look at a frame without playing, the game can play by itself for a number of ticks (60 a second), save the frame it ends on and exit. It is played by the doors player of the guard (the same file the tests use): it goes out to the newest door, picks up its applause and takes every encore's card and the program's by the players' one order. The capture's seed is one the guard plays, and on the committed numbers its performance reaches the ovation; the guard lets four seeds of twenty lose, so if a retuning makes this one lose, the capture is given a seed that wins. A card takes none of a capture's ticks, and the next act is gone on to at once; the ticks of every act's curtain are counted with the rest, sixty before each act, so on the committed numbers act n begins on tick 4560 × (n − 1).

The console is told the tick each encore, each program, the ovation or the closed stage is reached on (`Capture: Encore in act 1 on tick 410`), and a capture that ends on such a tick shows that screen. On the committed numbers: the first encore is on tick 410, with The Pierce among its three cards; 4560 is the first act's program, with its chorus card; 4575 is in the second act's curtain, with the first understudy on its way back under its caption; 12000 is the third act, with the magician at the second door; 19512 is the fifth, a crowd in the second doorway and the magician's cards, which have The Pierce by then, going on through it, with rival's understudies at the first door; 26554 is an encore in the sixth with all three doors open and The Burst and The Ricochet on offer (the frame shows the screen as it opens, with its leftmost card lit, and not the card the player then takes); 29000 is the seventh act, with the understudies holding the first and the second door; 39570 is the ninth act just after a crowd has come in at the first door, with the magician fallen; and 45600 is the ovation. That player takes The Burst in no encore of this performance, so no frame has its ring. Those are the frames of `art/frames/`. The ticks above move whenever a number or a rule moves what the doors player does: run a long capture and read the console for the new ones.

    dotnet run --project src/Understudies.Game -- --capture /tmp/frame.png --ticks 39570

A capture is silent: it makes no sound and opens no audio device.

The figures, the stage set and the two bursts of a card's effects are pixel-art sprites made with ludo.ai, read from `art/ludo/sprites/` (the repository's own when the game is run from its root, a copy beside the executable otherwise). `art/prompt-guide.md` has the recipe they were made by, and each `manifest.json` under `art/ludo/` the prompt and the settings of every generation.

## Tests

    dotnet test Understudies.sln --configuration Release

Three scripted players measure whether hiding at the box office loses and going out to the doors wins. The orbit player walks round the box office and never leaves its circle; it is played on three circles (on the box office, close round it, and five units out), and hiding has lost on a seed only when it lost on all three. The doors player holds the door that opened last, standing at it (a throw's range in, and no more than a third of the way to the box office), fetches applause up to its door and never into the doorway, steps back from a critic that is upon it, and goes back to the box office while a critic is at it. The kiter is the rival of going out: it walks round the box office just outside the floor where no fall earns applause, dodges as the doors player does, and steps out to a piece within nine units of its place on the circle, but to none that lies nearer an open door than the doors player's post there. All take an encore's card by one fixed order, and the program's card when there is one. Ordinary tests hold the committed `tuning.json` to the guard of the plan's decision 22 on two sets of seeds, 1 to 20, which the numbers were tuned on, and 101 to 120: the orbit player has to lose the box office by the end of act six, and the doors player to finish act ten, in at least 16 of the first set and 14 of the second; and the doors player has to do better than the kiter (finish at least as often, with half as many encores again and fifty more of the box office left). And two tripwires on the first set (plan T29): the doors player's box office at the end is at least 350 of 400 on average, and taking its cards by another order, the longer arm first, it still finishes on at least 16 of 20. On the committed numbers the doors player finishes on every seed with some 40 encores and the box office nearly whole (392 on average); the kiter does not lose either, with under half the encores and 337 of the box office. So hiding on the ring is poorer and does not lose. The order the cards are taken by decided both results before the three cards that change what a card does (plan T25) and decides less since: with the longer arm first the doors player finishes 20 of 20 and the kiter 19, and with "one more card" last, where nobody finished, both finish every seed, since the three stand in for it. The players' order has the three after "one more card", the damage card and the attack speed card and ahead of the longer arm and the quicker Vanish, which the players never took. The plan's T37, T29 and T25 have the tables, the open question and what the guard measures and what it does not. This prints the performances of the first set in full, how every one ended, each act's averages, its applause and its encores among them, and the guard's counts (it takes a few seconds, and asserts nothing):

    dotnet test tests/Understudies.Core.Tests --configuration Release --filter "FullyQualifiedName~ScriptedPlayersTests.PrintTheGuardsTable" --logger "console;verbosity=detailed"

For another budget, or any other variant of the tuning, there is `PrintTheVariants`, which plays the seeds 1 to 20 alone unless `UNDERSTUDIES_SEED_SETS` names other sets by their first seeds (`1,101`): numbers are searched on the first set only. `PrintTheCardOrders` plays the doors player and the kiter by eleven orders of taking cards: the committed one, its reverse, "one more card" last, the longer arm first, each of the three new cards first, each of them in the place of "one more card", and the three new cards last, as they are and made to do nothing.

Two more of the ordinary tests pin the state hash of a scripted performance: the same seed and the same player must end in the same state on macOS ARM and on Linux x64, and CI runs on both. A change to `tuning.json`, to a rule or to a player changes those hashes, and the tests' numbers are pinned again.
