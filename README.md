# The Understudies

A prototype of a short roguelite for PC. You are a stage magician defending the box office from critics. Each act lasts about a minute, and every act you play leaves behind a cardboard understudy that repeats the route you ran, in every act that follows. Where you run is what you build.

- `docs/vision.md` is the design.
- `docs/plan-prototype.md` is the plan of the prototype and its ticket list.

## Layout

- `src/Understudies.Core`: the rules of the game, with no engine dependency.
- `src/Understudies.Game`: the MonoGame layer (window, input, drawing, sound).
- `tests/Understudies.Core.Tests`: NUnit tests for Core.

## Requirements

The .NET SDK named in `global.json` (10.0.1xx). Nothing else: MonoGame and the text library come from NuGet.

## Running

    dotnet run --project src/Understudies.Game

WASD, the arrows or a gamepad's left stick walk the magician. The dark band along the top of the stage is the back wall: the floor starts at its foot, and the magician cannot walk onto it. Critics come in by the lit doors and walk to the box office to strike it. A critic the magician comes near turns on the magician instead, walks at it and hurts it by touching it; the moment the magician is out of its reach it goes back to the box office. Nothing blocks the magician: it walks through critics. The magician throws cards by itself, at the nearest critic in range, and a critic that has taken enough of them falls. Space, or a gamepad's A, is the Vanish: the magician is at once a few steps further the way it walks (the way it last walked, when it stands), for a moment nothing hurts it, and where it stood it leaves a cloud that stuns the critics it touches: a stunned critic turns pale and neither walks nor hurts anything for a moment. The small bar over the magician's head fills as the Vanish comes back; a press before it is full does nothing. The small bar under the magician's feet is what the magician has left, and the bar above the box office, with the number beside it, what the box office has left. When the box office's runs out the show closes and the stage goes dark. When the magician's runs out the magician falls, and the show goes on: it lies flat where it fell and takes no more keys for the rest of the act, no critic minds it any more, a line on the back wall says so, and the understudies play the act to its end. The next act starts with the magician whole and on its mark. The act it fell in was recorded only as far as the fall: in every later act that act's understudy plays up to that moment and is then gone from the stage.

A critic that falls to a card the magician itself threw leaves applause where it fell: a bright pink diamond, in a colour nothing else on the stage has. An understudy's card leaves none, whoever hurt the critic before it. The magician picks a piece up by being on it, walking over it or standing where it falls, and nobody else can: an understudy walks over applause and leaves it, and so does nothing for a magician that has fallen. A piece lies for four seconds (`applauseTime`), dimming all the while, and is then gone. The pink bar at the foot of the back wall is the act's applause: the pieces picked up in this act as a share of the critics let in so far in this act, so it sinks a little with every critic that enters. Its two notches are the two thresholds (`applauseFirstThreshold` and `applauseSecondThreshold`, 15 and 35 in a hundred), and the second stands seven tenths of the way along the bar. Nothing is bought with applause yet: the cards it pays for come with the program. Applause is not kept: going on to the next act clears the floor and empties the bar.

A blow is shown as well as counted. A critic that a card hurts flashes white; one that falls bursts into scraps of paper and lies where it fell, fading. A thrown card trails a streak. The magician flashes when a touch hurts it, the box office when it is struck, and the whole stage shakes with either: a little for one blow, more under a crowd's, and no more than that however big the crowd. A Vanish holds everything still for a moment and puffs scraps where the magician left and where it arrives, and a piece of applause that is picked up puffs a few where it lay. None of this is a rule, so its numbers are not in `tuning.json`: they are the constants at the top of `src/Understudies.Game/Juice.cs`, and a changed one needs the game started again.

A blow is heard too. Seven short sounds are worked out in code when the game starts, and there is no sound file: a flick for a thrown card, a dry tap for a card that hurts a critic, a falling tone with paper tearing in it for a critic that falls, a whoosh for the Vanish, a low thump for a strike on the box office, a buzz when a critic hurts the magician, and a short bright chime for a piece of applause picked up (one that is dropped makes no sound). A sound comes a little from the side of the stage it happened on, is a little higher or lower each time, and does not start again within a twentieth of a second of its own last start, however many blows a crowd deals in that time. M mutes the sound and M again brings it back; a sound that has started rings out. A machine with no audio device runs the game silent and says so on the console. The numbers of the sound (how loud each is, the gap between two starts, every recipe) are the constants at the top of `src/Understudies.Game/Sound.cs`, and a changed one needs the game started again.

A performance is ten acts of seventy-five seconds (`actsInPerformance` and `actLength` in `tuning.json`); the back wall says which act it is and how much of its time is left. An act ends when its time runs out, whatever is on the stage. The stage then stands, while what was in the air settles, until Enter, or a gamepad's Start, goes on to the next act: the magician begins it whole and on its mark, and the critics of the last act are still where they stood. Every act, the first too, begins with the curtain: one second (`curtainTime`) in which the stage stands, the act's time does not run and no key is taken, a press of the Vanish included. In that second every understudy slides back along its own route, from where it stood when the last act ended to where its route starts, fast at first and settling; and through the curtain of the second act and the first three seconds of that act, and never again in that performance, one line over the first understudy's head, going where it goes, says what it is. Every act that was played leaves an understudy, from the next act on: a pale, half-transparent copy of the magician in the colour of its act, with the line of its route faint on the floor. It stands, tick for tick, where the magician stood in that act, leaves a cloud where and when the magician vanished, and throws cards (a little duller than the magician's own) at whatever critic is in its range now. Nothing hurts, pushes or stops it, and the critics take no notice of it. After the last act the performance ends in a standing ovation. R starts a new performance, at any time. Esc quits.

Who enters when is planned for the whole performance when it starts, from its seed. Every act has a budget (`firstActBudget`, and `budgetGrowthPerAct` more with every act) and buys enemies with it from `enemyKinds`, each kind at its `cost`, by its `weight` among the kinds the act may have (`fromAct`) and can still afford, until it can afford none. There is one kind, the critic, at a cost of 1: the first act has twenty-five critics and the tenth ninety-seven. An act's critics are spread evenly over it, each a little earlier or later by chance, and nobody enters in its last seconds (`actQuietEnd`) or while its curtain is up. Each enters by one of the doors that are open in that act: a stage door opens in the act `opensInAct` names and stays open, the first in act one, the second in act three and the third in act six. An open door is lit and a shut one dim, from the act's curtain on, and the back wall says, beside the act's number, how many critics are still to come in this act.

The words are drawn in a system font found at start-up: Arial on macOS and Windows, DejaVu Sans or Liberation Sans on Linux. On a machine with none of them the game runs without its words and says so on the console.

Every number of the rules is in `tuning.json` at the repository root. The game reads the `tuning.json` of the directory it is started from, so run it from the root, and the copy beside the executable when there is none. Edit the file and press F5 (fn+F5 on a Mac's built-in keyboard): the game goes on with the new numbers. What an act starts with (its length, its curtain's, the magician's mark and hit points) counts from the next act, and the box office's hit points from the next performance, so press R as well; a countdown that is already running (the curtain's time, the act's time, a critic's next blow, the next throw, the Vanish coming back, a cloud's time, a stun, a piece of applause's time) runs out at its old length first. A critic already on the stage keeps the hit points it entered with, and a card already in the air the range it was thrown with. The waves are not planned again: who enters when, by which door and of which kind was settled when the performance started, so new budgets, kinds' costs and weights and `actQuietEnd` count from the next performance (R), and so do doors' acts for who enters where. A planned entry names its door and its kind by their places in `stageDoors` and `enemyKinds`, so those lists are not to be reordered or shortened in a performance under way. Three more things a reload does to a performance under way: more `actsInPerformance` gives the added acts no critics; a shorter `actLength` leaves out the entries planned past the act's new end; and a door's new `opensInAct` lights or dims the door at once, while the critics still enter by the doors they were planned for. A key the game does not know or a key that is missing is an error that names the key, and so is a stage door above `stageFloorTop`, the foot of the back wall (the third door stands at that foot, so the two numbers change together), no door open in act one, no kind of enemy, and a kind whose `cost` is less than 1; the console shows it, and the game keeps the numbers it had (or, at start-up, does not start).

To look at a frame without playing, the game can walk a fixed script for a number of ticks (60 a second), save the frame it ends on and exit. The script keeps out of the way while critics gather at the box office, walks back to them and stands while they turn on the magician, vanishes when thirty-one seconds of the act have gone by, and stands where the Vanish took it, throwing at the crowd, until the act is over. In the second act it walks out to the first door and fells every critic that enters, and in the third it goes round the back of the box office and stands on its far side; in every later act it stands on its mark, and between two acts it goes on by itself. The ticks of every act's curtain are counted with the rest, sixty before each act. So 1890 ticks end with the crowd round the magician and its hit points going, 1930 a sixth of a second after the Vanish with its scraps in the air and the crowd pale in its cloud, 1960 two thirds of a second after the Vanish, 2760 with four pieces of applause on the floor, two fresh and two dim, and the bar part full of the two the magician happened to stand by, 4560 on the last tick of the first act, with the stage standing between two acts, 4575 a quarter of the way through the curtain of the second act, with the first understudy on its way back under its caption, 6520 in the second act with the first act's understudy two thirds of a second after its Vanish, 9135 a quarter of the way through the curtain of the third act, with two understudies part-way back along their routes, 10721 in the third act with two doors lit, two understudies on two routes and a card of one of them in the air, 24872 in the sixth with all three doors lit and a critic just in by each, and 45600 in the standing ovation:

    dotnet run --project src/Understudies.Game -- --capture /tmp/frame.png --ticks 1890

A capture is silent: it makes no sound and opens no audio device.

The script never closes the show and never lets the magician fall. To see either, run the capture from a directory whose `tuning.json` is a changed copy. With 60 `boxOfficeHitPoints` the box office has fallen by tick 1560. With 20 `magicianHitPoints` and 100000 `boxOfficeHitPoints` (a crowd that nobody thins would close the show) the magician falls in the first act on tick 1939, stands through the second, and falls in the third on tick 9503 and in every act after it: 9600 is in the third act with the magician down and the two understudies playing on, and 14140 in the fourth, with the magician down again, where the third act's understudy has left the stage at the end of its short route and the two older ones still play.
