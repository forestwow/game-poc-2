# The Understudies

A prototype of a short roguelite for PC. You are a stage magician defending the box office from critics. Each act lasts about a minute, and every act you play leaves behind a cardboard understudy that repeats the route you ran, in every act that follows. Where you run is what you build.

- `docs/vision.md` is the design.
- `docs/plan-prototype.md` is the plan of the prototype and its ticket list.

## Layout

- `src/Understudies.Core`: the rules of the game, with no engine dependency.
- `src/Understudies.Game`: the MonoGame layer (window, input, drawing, sound).
- `tests/Understudies.Core.Tests`: NUnit tests for Core.

## Requirements

The .NET SDK named in `global.json` (10.0.1xx). Nothing else: MonoGame and the text library come from NuGet.

## Running

    dotnet run --project src/Understudies.Game

WASD, the arrows or a gamepad's left stick walk the magician. The dark band along the top of the stage is the back wall: the floor starts at its foot, and the magician cannot walk onto it. Critics come in by the lit doors and walk to the box office to strike it. A critic the magician comes near turns on the magician instead, walks at it and hurts it by touching it; the moment the magician is out of its reach it goes back to the box office. From the fourth act on stagehands come in with the critics: smaller, rust where a critic is blue, four times as fast, and felled by one card. A stagehand never turns on the magician: it runs to the box office whoever stands in its way, and strikes it as a critic does. Nothing blocks the magician: it walks through critics. The magician throws cards by itself, at the nearest critic or stagehand in range, and whoever has taken enough of them falls. A card is thrown ahead of a target that moves, at where the two will meet if the target goes on as it goes; it flies straight, so one that stops or turns while the card is in the air may be missed. Space, or a gamepad's A, is the Vanish: the magician is at once a few steps further the way it walks (the way it last walked, when it stands), for a moment nothing hurts it, and where it stood it leaves a cloud that stuns the critics it touches: a stunned critic turns pale and neither walks nor hurts anything for a moment. The small bar over the magician's head fills as the Vanish comes back; a press before it is full does nothing. The small bar under the magician's feet is what the magician has left, and the bar above the box office, with the number beside it, what the box office has left. When the box office's runs out the show closes and the stage goes dark. When the magician's runs out the magician falls, and the show goes on: it lies flat where it fell and takes no more keys for the rest of the act, no critic minds it any more, a line on the back wall says so, and the understudies play the act to its end. The next act starts with the magician whole and on its mark. The act it fell in was recorded only as far as the fall: in every later act that act's understudy plays up to that moment and is then gone from the stage.

A critic that falls to a card the magician itself threw leaves applause where it fell: a bright pink diamond, in a colour nothing else on the stage has. An understudy's card leaves none, whoever hurt the critic before it. The magician picks a piece up by coming near it (within `applausePickUpReach` of its own circle, which also takes a piece dropped by a critic that fell touching it), and nobody else can: an understudy walks over applause and leaves it, and so does nothing for a magician that has fallen. A piece lies for four seconds (`applauseTime`), dimming all the while, and is then gone. The pink bar at the foot of the back wall is the act's applause: the pieces picked up in this act as a share of the critics let in so far in this act, so it sinks a little with every critic that enters. Its two notches are the two thresholds (`applauseFirstThreshold` and `applauseSecondThreshold`, 15 and 35 in a hundred), and the second stands seven tenths of the way along the bar. Applause pays for the cards of the program that follows the act, and is not kept: going on to the next act clears the floor and empties the bar.

When an act is over and another follows, its applause is paid in cards: the program. An act that picked up nothing has no program and no card. A share under the first notch offers one card, with no choice; one that has reached the first notch a choice of two; one that has reached the second a choice of three, each of which has a chance (`cardChorusChance`, one in five) of being the chorus card, one in an offer at most. No card is offered twice in one program, and which cards are offered follows from the performance's seed. The program is a screen of its own: the stage stands and is dimmed a little, with the critics left on it still seen, and the offer is paper cards side by side across the front of the floor. Each card has a band along its top that says whose it is, yellow for the magician and green for every understudy, with its place at the band's left end; under it its name, what one of it changes in the numbers of `tuning.json`, and how many of it are already held. The highlighted card is the bright one in a white frame, a little higher than the others, and it is the leftmost when the program opens. The left and right arrows, A and D, a gamepad's d-pad or its left stick move the highlight, and Enter, Space or the gamepad's A take the highlighted card; the keys 1, 2 and 3 take the card in that place at once. Only a press counts: a key or a stick held since the act (Space for a Vanish, D to walk) does nothing until it has been let go. Under the cards the program's twelve seconds (`programTime`) run down as a number and as a bar, with the words for what the end of them does: the leftmost card is taken. A card that is taken, by a key or by the clock, stays lit for most of a second with a chime, the others faint beside it and the back wall naming it, and in that moment Enter does not go on. Then the stage is between two acts, as after an act with no program. In the program and between two acts a line under the applause bar says what the act's applause came to (no applause and no card, one card and no choice, the first notch and a choice of two, the second and a choice of three), and a line at the stage's bottom edge what is held so far in this performance. There are six cards, and any of them may be taken again and adds again. Five are self cards, which change the magician from then on: sharper cards (a thrown card takes `cardDamage`, half a hit point, more), quicker hands (throws come `cardAttackSpeed`, a fifth, more often: the time between two is the `throwCooldown` over 1.2, and over 1.4 with two), a longer arm (the throw reaches `cardRange`, a unit and a half, further, and its cards fly that far), a quicker Vanish (it comes back `cardVanishCooldown`, a quarter, faster, counted the same way: its wait is the `vanishCooldown` over 1.25, a fifth shorter) and one more card (each throw sends one more, at the next nearest enemy in range: with one enemy in range, one card). An understudy is the magician of the act it was recorded in: it throws for ever with the self cards the magician had when that act began, and a card taken later changes no understudy that is already there. The sixth is the chorus card: every understudy's cards, those there are and those there will be, take `cardChorusDamage`, half a hit point, more, on top of what each has of its own, and the magician's own cards do not.

A blow is shown as well as counted. A critic that a card hurts flashes white; one that falls bursts into scraps of paper and lies where it fell, fading. A thrown card spins and trails a streak, with a flick of light at the hand that threw it; where it strikes there is a burst of its suits, and where its critic falls a splash of ink and newsprint. The magician flashes when a touch hurts it, the box office when it is struck, and the whole stage shakes with either: a little for one blow, more under a crowd's, and no more than that however big the crowd. A Vanish holds everything still for a moment and puffs scraps where the magician left and where it arrives, and a piece of applause that is picked up puffs a few where it lay. None of this is a rule, so its numbers are not in `tuning.json`: they are the constants at the top of `src/Understudies.Game/Juice.cs`, and a changed one needs the game started again.

A blow is heard too. Seven short sounds are worked out in code when the game starts, and there is no sound file: a flick for a thrown card, a dry tap for a card that hurts a critic, a falling tone with paper tearing in it for a critic that falls, a whoosh for the Vanish, a low thump for a strike on the box office, a buzz when a critic hurts the magician, and a short bright chime for a piece of applause picked up (one that is dropped makes no sound), which is heard for a card taken from the program too. A sound comes a little from the side of the stage it happened on, is a little higher or lower each time, and does not start again within a twentieth of a second of its own last start, however many blows a crowd deals in that time. M mutes the sound and M again brings it back; a sound that has started rings out. A machine with no audio device runs the game silent and says so on the console. The numbers of the sound (how loud each is, the gap between two starts, every recipe) are the constants at the top of `src/Understudies.Game/Sound.cs`, and a changed one needs the game started again.

A performance is ten acts of seventy-five seconds (`actsInPerformance` and `actLength` in `tuning.json`); the back wall says which act it is and how much of its time is left. An act ends when its time runs out, whatever is on the stage. The stage then stands, while what was in the air settles: first for the program, if the act earned one, until a card is taken, and then until Enter, or a gamepad's Start, goes on to the next act: the magician begins it whole and on its mark, and the critics of the last act are still where they stood. Every act, the first too, begins with the curtain: one second (`curtainTime`) in which the stage stands, the act's time does not run and no key is taken, a press of the Vanish included. In that second every understudy slides back along its own route, from where it stood when the last act ended to where its route starts, fast at first and settling; and through the curtain of the second act and the first three seconds of that act, and never again in that performance, one line over the first understudy's head, going where it goes, says what it is. Every act that was played leaves an understudy, from the next act on: a pale, half-transparent copy of the magician in the colour of its act, with the line of its route faint on the floor. It stands, tick for tick, where the magician stood in that act, leaves a cloud where and when the magician vanished, and throws cards (a little duller than the magician's own) at whatever critic is in its range now. Nothing hurts, pushes or stops it, and the critics take no notice of it. After the last act the performance ends in a standing ovation. R starts a new performance, at any time. Esc quits.

Who enters when is planned for the whole performance when it starts, from its seed. Every act has a budget (`firstActBudget`, and `budgetGrowthPerAct` more with every act) and buys enemies with it from `enemyKinds`, each kind at its `cost`, by its `weight` among the kinds the act may have (`fromAct`) and can still afford, until it can afford none. There are two kinds, the critic and, from the fourth act, the stagehand, each at a cost of 1, and a critic is bought three times as readily: the first act has fifteen critics, the second ninety-five, and the tenth seven hundred and thirty-five enemies, about one in four of them a stagehand. An act's critics are spread evenly over it, each a little earlier or later by chance, and nobody enters in its last seconds (`actQuietEnd`) or while its curtain is up. Each enters by one of the doors that are open in that act: a stage door opens in the act `opensInAct` names and stays open, the first in act one, the second in act three and the third in act six. An open door is lit and a shut one dim, from the act's curtain on, and the back wall says, beside the act's number, how many critics are still to come in this act.

The words are drawn in a system font found at start-up: Arial on macOS and Windows, DejaVu Sans or Liberation Sans on Linux. On a machine with none of them the game runs without its words and says so on the console.

Every number of the rules is in `tuning.json` at the repository root. The game reads the `tuning.json` of the directory it is started from, so run it from the root, and the copy beside the executable when there is none. Edit the file and press F5 (fn+F5 on a Mac's built-in keyboard): the game goes on with the new numbers. What an act starts with (its length, its curtain's, the magician's mark and hit points) counts from the next act, and the box office's hit points from the next performance, so press R as well; a countdown that is already running (the curtain's time, the act's time, a critic's next blow, the next throw, the Vanish coming back, a cloud's time, a stun, a piece of applause's time, the program's time) runs out at its old length first. A critic already on the stage keeps the hit points it entered with, and a card already in the air the range and the damage it was thrown with. What a card of the program gives counts at once for every card of that name already taken, the understudies' too: the magician and each understudy keep how many of each card they have, and not the numbers that came of them. The waves are not planned again: who enters when, by which door and of which kind was settled when the performance started, so new budgets, kinds' costs and weights and `actQuietEnd` count from the next performance (R), and so do doors' acts for who enters where. A planned entry names its door and its kind by their places in `stageDoors` and `enemyKinds`, so those lists are not to be reordered or shortened in a performance under way. Three more things a reload does to a performance under way: more `actsInPerformance` gives the added acts no critics; a shorter `actLength` leaves out the entries planned past the act's new end; and a door's new `opensInAct` lights or dims the door at once, while the critics still enter by the doors they were planned for. A key the game does not know or a key that is missing is an error that names the key, and so is a stage door above `stageFloorTop`, the foot of the back wall (the third door stands at that foot, so the two numbers change together), no door open in act one, no kind of enemy, and a kind whose `cost` is less than 1; the console shows it, and the game keeps the numbers it had (or, at start-up, does not start).

To look at a frame without playing, the game can walk a fixed script for a number of ticks (60 a second), save the frame it ends on and exit. The script is a poor player. In the first act it keeps out of the way while critics gather at the box office, walks back to them and stands while they turn on the magician, vanishes to the left when thirty-one seconds of the act have gone by, and stands where the Vanish took it. In the second act it walks out to the first door, and in the third it goes round the back of the box office and stands on its far side; in every later act it stands on its mark. A program's leftmost card it takes at once, so a program takes none of the capture's ticks, and between two acts it goes on by itself. The ticks of every act's curtain are counted with the rest, sixty before each act.

On the committed numbers the script loses, and a capture shows the first act and the start of the second. The first critic strikes the box office on tick 992. 1890 ticks end with critics on the magician, three of its eight hit points left and the applause bar part full: the piece of the critic that fell beside it was picked up as it dropped; the Vanish is on tick 1921, and 1930 is a sixth of a second after it, with two critics pale in its cloud and the magician, six units to the left, at its last hit point; on tick 1939 a critic fells it, and 1960 has the magician down, the cloud thinning and the two still pale. From there the critics have the box office to themselves: 2760 has five of them at it, and 4560, the last tick of the first act, has the stage standing in that act's program, its one card (a damage card, for the one piece the magician picked up) on the dimmed stage with twelve seconds under it, thirteen critics left and 117 of the box office's 400. 4575 is a quarter of the way through the curtain of the second act, with the first understudy on its way back under its caption and the magician whole on its mark, and 4700 is in the second act, with the magician on its way out to the first door and nine critics at the box office. The show closes on tick 5291, and every longer capture shows the closed stage:

    dotnet run --project src/Understudies.Game -- --capture /tmp/frame.png --ticks 1890

A capture is silent: it makes no sound and opens no audio device.

The figures, the stage set and the two bursts of a card's effects are pixel-art sprites made with ludo.ai, read from `art/ludo/sprites/` (the repository's own when the game is run from its root, a copy beside the executable otherwise). `art/prompt-guide.md` has the recipe they were made by, and each `manifest.json` under `art/ludo/` the prompt and the settings of every generation.

The script was written on the numbers of before plan T20, on which it played to the ovation: on the committed ones it loses in the second act, so no capture reaches an understudy's Vanish, the third act, the third door or the ovation. A script that plays the committed numbers is a ticket of its own.

## Tests

    dotnet test Understudies.sln --configuration Release

Two scripted players measure whether hiding at the box office loses and going out to the doors wins. The orbit player walks round the box office and never leaves its circle; it is played on three circles (on the box office, close round it, and five units out), and hiding has lost on a seed only when it lost on all three. The doors player holds the door that opened last, steps back from a critic that is upon it, and goes back to the box office while a critic is at it. The committed `tuning.json` is tuned to the guard of the plan's decision 22, and an ordinary test holds it there: over the seeds 1 to 20 the orbit player has to lose the box office by the end of act six in at least 16, and the doors player to finish act ten in at least 16. This prints the same performances in full, how every one ended, each act's averages and the two counts (it takes a few seconds, and asserts nothing):

    dotnet test tests/Understudies.Core.Tests --configuration Release --filter "FullyQualifiedName~ScriptedPlayersTests.PrintTheGuardsTable" --logger "console;verbosity=detailed"

With `PrintTheTableOnAnotherBudget` in the place of `PrintTheGuardsTable` it prints the same on three other budgets, with nothing else changed.

Two more of the ordinary tests pin the state hash of a scripted performance: the same seed and the same player must end in the same state on macOS ARM and on Linux x64. A change to `tuning.json`, to a rule or to a player changes those hashes, and the tests' numbers are pinned again.
